# Music through the medicine eras

Each loop is an original chiptune sketch rather than a reconstruction of a surviving historical recording. The references are prompts for interval, rhythm and instrumentation, and the danger version keeps each era's identity while making the bottle feel closer to overflowing.

## Protomedicine

The protomedicine loop uses an A-minor pentatonic call-and-response, a slow A/E drone and frame-drum toms: a compact convention for ritual repetition and the shaman's pre-literate setting, not a claim about what prehistoric medicine sounded like. No single prehistoric musical practice can stand in for every society, so the hook is intentionally abstract ([Prehistoric music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_music)).

## Egyptian

The Egyptian loop uses an E double-harmonic colour, stepwise leading tones and a clipped fifth-based bass beneath a papyrus-like pulse. Calling that scale “Egyptian” is a modern Hollywood/Orientalist convention rather than evidence of notated Egyptian music; no notated ancient Egyptian repertoire survives to validate this exact sound ([Music of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_ancient_Egypt)).

## Hippocratic

The Hippocratic loop is a Dorian lyre gesture built from broken D minor, C, A minor and G shapes, with a measured pulse for the case-writing physician. Dorian mode is a modern analytical label applied here as a musical shorthand, not proof that a physician on Kos used this exact harmony ([Hippocratic Corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocratic_Corpus), [Dorian mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorian_mode)).

## Bimaristan

The bimaristan loop uses a D Hijaz ornament, a D drone with G answers and darbuka-like kick, hat and tom figures to suggest an Abbasid-era ward where translated books and pharmacies met. Hijaz is a real maqam tradition in Arabic music, but this short synthesized melody is a present-day game convention rather than a reconstruction of Baghdad's sound ([Bimaristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimaristan), [Maqam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maqam_(music))).

## Apothecary

The apothecary loop sets a G Dorian triangle melody over organum-like parallel fifths and a regular tabor. The fifths are a deliberate reference to medieval European organum from the ninth through thirteenth centuries, while the apothecary's mortar-and-jar world spans more places and practices than one melody can represent ([Organum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organum), [Apothecary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apothecary)).

## Plague

The plague loop descends slowly through D minor over a D/A pedal and a sparse funeral drum with a pickup at the end of the bar. The association belongs to the era's quarantine, mortality and later plague-doctor imagery, not to a documented “plague music” genre; the historical costume itself is more specific to early modern Europe than the medieval pandemic ([Plague doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_doctor), [Quarantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarantine)).

## Patent Medicine

The patent-medicine loop is a bright, syncopated C-major rag with stride-like bass and a tabor pulse, reflecting the theatrical salesmanship of nineteenth-century tonics. Ragtime's strongest period was roughly the 1890s through the 1910s, which fits inside this game's 1800–1906 band as a stylized overlap rather than a claim that every patent remedy used ragtime ([Patent medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent_medicine), [Ragtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragtime)).

## Antisepsis

The antisepsis loop is an F-major 3/4 waltz, with triangle phrases in twelve sixteenths and a bass that marks the first beat before answering on beats two and three. The Viennese waltz is a useful convention for the social and industrial world surrounding the 1867–1927 germ-theory era, not a sound uniquely belonging to operating theatres ([Joseph Lister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Lister), [Waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltz)).

## Pharmaceutical

The pharmaceutical loop keeps RxDrop's original calm melody and its separate danger arrangement: clean sustained phrases when the bottle is under control, then a tighter, brighter fever version when the neck fills. Its association is with industrial pharmaceutical production and standardized dosage, not with a single national musical style ([Pharmaceutical industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_industry)).

## Genetic

The genetic loop turns an A-minor arpeggio through F, C and G into a precise sixteenth-note sequence, with a sawtooth eighth-note bass and hats that suggest a sequencer. The result is deliberately techno-coded: a contemporary convention for genomics, computation and engineered therapies rather than a prediction of future laboratory music ([Genomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genomics), [Techno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno)).
